# Mentorship Expectations

## 1. What do you expect from this mentorship?

My primary expectation from this mentorship is to develop the knowledge, skills, and engineering mindset required to become an independent Software Engineer.

I want to learn how to approach unfamiliar problems systematically, starting from understanding the problem, analyzing requirements, identifying constraints, breaking down complex problems, and developing appropriate solutions before translating them into code.

One of my main areas of focus is improving my computational thinking and problem-solving abilities. I want to understand how experienced Software Engineers think, analyze problems, structure their reasoning, evaluate different approaches, and make technical decisions.

In addition, I hope to gain practical experience through case studies, technical discussions, code reviews, problem-solving exercises, and project implementation.

Ultimately, I want to become an engineer who can independently understand problems, determine appropriate solutions, implement them, and take responsibility for the results rather than depending too heavily on instructions, existing examples, or AI-generated solutions.

By learning and developing competencies in these areas, I hope to become an industry-ready Software Engineer who has the knowledge, skills, and mindset to contribute effectively in a professional working environment.

## 2. What do you expect from yourself?

I expect myself to become an active, disciplined, and responsible mentee who takes ownership of my learning process.

I want to develop the habit of preparing before each mentoring session by studying relevant materials, exploring concepts independently, practicing problem-solving, and documenting questions or difficulties I encounter.

I also want to challenge myself to think before writing code. Instead of immediately searching for implementations or asking AI for solutions, I want to practice understanding the problem, identifying requirements, designing logical steps, and evaluating possible approaches.

Furthermore, I want to become more comfortable explaining my reasoning, defending my technical decisions, receiving criticism, and learning from mistakes.

My goal is not simply to complete assignments or follow instructions, but to gradually develop the ability to solve problems independently and take responsibility for my own technical growth.

## 3. What do you normally do when you don't understand something?

### My previous habits and challenges:

In the past, I tended to ask AI directly whenever I encountered something I did not understand. I often looked for immediate answers without first identifying the root cause, analyzing the problem, or exploring possible solutions independently.

Although I became familiar with programming syntax and implementing APIs through previous learning experiences, I realized that I still struggled when I had to solve unfamiliar problems independently.

In particular, I often found it difficult to translate business requirements into logical steps, design algorithms, and determine how those solutions should be implemented in code.

As a result, I sometimes relied on AI-generated explanations or code examples without fully understanding the reasoning and problem-solving process behind them.

### Steps I want to take to improve:

When I encounter a problem, I want to develop the habit of understanding and analyzing it before looking for an implementation.

I want to identify the actual problem, understand its context and requirements, break it down into smaller parts, identify relevant conditions, and develop a logical approach to solving it.

For technical problems, I want to practice creating algorithms, pseudocode, or flowcharts before translating them into code.

I still want to use AI as a learning assistant to discuss ideas, evaluate my reasoning, explore alternative solutions, and clarify difficult concepts. However, I do not want AI to replace my own thinking and decision-making process.

If I cannot solve a problem independently, I want to bring my analysis, attempted solutions, and unresolved questions to discuss with my mentor.

## 4. How do you usually learn a technology you have never used?

### My previous habits and challenges:

In the past, I tended to become interested in learning programming languages or technologies based on their popularity and potential salary opportunities.

Although this encouraged me to explore different technologies, I realized that I sometimes focused too much on learning tools without fully understanding the problems they were designed to solve.

I also noticed that following tutorials and implementation examples could make me familiar with a technology without necessarily developing a deeper understanding of its underlying concepts.

### Steps I want to take to improve:

When learning a new technology, I want to start by understanding its purpose, fundamental concepts, underlying principles, and the problems it is designed to solve.

After that, I will study its official documentation, explore important concepts, and experiment with simple implementations.

I also want to understand how the technology works internally, how it interacts with other system components, and what considerations should be taken into account when using it.

Most importantly, I want to apply what I learn to practical problems rather than simply following tutorials.

For me, successfully learning a technology means being able to explain its concepts, understand when and why it should be used, implement it appropriately, and evaluate the consequences of my technical decisions.

## 5. How do you determine whether information from the internet is reliable?

### My previous habits and challenges:

In the past, I tended to trust AI-generated answers without carefully checking their sources or verifying whether the information was accurate and relevant to my situation.

I sometimes applied suggested solutions immediately because I wanted to solve problems quickly, without considering whether the approach was appropriate for the specific context.

I realize that this habit could affect my ability to evaluate information critically and make independent technical decisions.

### Steps I want to take to improve:

I want to develop the habit of evaluating information before accepting or applying it.

First, I will prioritize reliable sources such as official documentation, technical references, reputable publications, and credible engineering discussions.

Second, I will compare information from multiple sources and examine whether their explanations are consistent.

Third, I will consider the context, including technology versions, implementation requirements, limitations, and the specific problem being addressed.

Finally, whenever possible, I will verify the information through practical experiments, testing, or implementation.

I also want to use AI-generated information critically by treating it as a starting point for further investigation rather than an unquestionable answer.

## 6. What would you do if you disagreed with your mentor?

If I disagreed with my mentor, I would express my opinion through open discussion and provide clear reasoning to support my perspective.

I would try to explain my analysis, assumptions, and the references behind my opinion. At the same time, I would listen carefully to my mentor's reasoning and understand the considerations behind their approach.

I believe disagreements can provide valuable opportunities to examine problems from different perspectives and improve my understanding.

I also want to develop the ability to evaluate arguments objectively rather than simply defending my own opinions.

My goal is not to prove that I am right, but to understand the problem more deeply and identify the most appropriate solution based on reasoning, evidence, and engineering considerations.

## 7. What does "I understand this topic" mean to you?

For me, understanding a topic means more than remembering definitions, recognizing syntax, or following implementation examples from tutorials.

I believe I truly understand a topic when I am able to:

- Explain the concept using my own understanding and words.
- Understand the underlying principles and why a particular concept or approach is used.
- Apply the concept to a problem or situation I have not encountered before.
- Analyze problems and translate requirements into logical steps.
- Develop an appropriate solution before implementing it in code.
- Implement the solution independently without constantly relying on existing examples or AI-generated code.
- Identify errors, evaluate possible solutions, and understand the trade-offs of different approaches.
- Explain and justify my technical decisions with clear reasoning.

I also want to evaluate my understanding through practical exercises, case studies, project implementation, technical discussions, and code reviews.

Therefore, I do not measure my understanding only by whether I can make the code work, but also by whether I can independently understand the problem, develop the solution, explain my reasoning, and take responsibility for the implementation.

## 8. What do you think separates someone who can code from someone who can work as a Software Engineer?

In my opinion, someone who can code generally has the ability to translate instructions or predefined requirements into executable code.

A Software Engineer, however, needs to understand the actual problem before deciding how to solve it.

This involves analyzing requirements, identifying constraints, breaking down complex problems, developing logical solutions, evaluating alternatives, and making technical decisions based on appropriate reasoning.

A Software Engineer should also be able to translate business requirements into system behavior and implement solutions that are reliable, maintainable, secure, and appropriate for their intended purpose.

Beyond implementation skills, an engineer needs to understand software architecture, data structures, databases, security, testing, performance, and how different system components interact.

I also believe that structured thinking, problem-solving, critical thinking, communication, collaboration, and the ability to learn independently are essential qualities.

Ultimately, I want to become a Software Engineer who can confidently face unfamiliar problems, think systematically, find appropriate solutions, implement them independently, and take responsibility for the systems and decisions I make.
